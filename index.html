Estimate political actor positions — and take the data with you.

PIP Indices is an interactive database for comparative political research, built on the PIP — Parties, Institutions & Preferences project. It provides indicators on political parties, governments, institutions and political preferences for 36 countries and the European Union from 1945 to the present, for researchers, political scientists, journalists and other users interested in comparative politics.

Pick a scale and model, choose your countries and time span, then run it in your browser. No install, no server — the computation runs locally via WebR.

What it is

This site publishes the PIP — Parties, Institutions & Preferences data collection. Its core is institutional data collected by the project itself, hand-coded from national sources and maintained as a quarterly time series. Ideological preferences are added from the Manifesto Project (MARPOR) data (see source note below).

What the project collects itself

Governments
Cabinet composition quarter by quarter — governing parties, seat shares, individual ministerial portfolios, majority or minority status.
Veto players
The institutional actors able to block policy change: parliamentary chambers, presidents, and — for member states — the EU, each with its own ideological position and range.
Party characteristics
Party histories (mergers, splits and renamings tracked under stable party codes), ideological cohesion, and ideological importance — how much weight a party actually gives a dimension — on both the Left–Right and the Green–Growth scale. The Manifesto Project's own RILE index is carried alongside them for comparison.
EU institutions
Positions of the European Council, the Commission, the Council of Ministers and the European Parliament, plus an overall EU position weighted by the decision rules of each treaty period.
Ideological misfit
The distance between a member state's government and the EU position on the same scale in the same quarter — how far a national government sits from the EU it is bound by.

Analytically derived, not assembled ad hoc

Every institutional variable here follows from explicit analytical and theoretical reasoning about what the concept is meant to capture — how a government position should be aggregated, what makes an actor a veto player, how salience and cohesion are defined. In most cases that derivation has been set out and reviewed in a peer-reviewed journal article (listed under Documentation & publications). This is what makes the measures offered here analytically defined indices for macro-comparative research rather than ad-hoc codings: they mean the same thing across 36 countries and across seven decades, which is the precondition for comparing them at all.

Also for journalists and practitioners

You do not need to be doing macro-quantitative research to get something out of this. The same data answers plain factual questions about institutions and party programmes: who governed a country and how in any given quarter and with which parties, whether a cabinet had a majority and which constraints it is facing (veto players), how a party's programme moved over the decades, and how far a national government stood from the EU. For the EU itself there are data on the European Council, the Commission, the Council of Ministers and the European Parliament. Everything is quarterly, covers 1945 to the present, and downloads as a plain spreadsheet — no account, no licence, no statistical software required.

Ideological preferences: source

How to use this tool

This tool estimates where political actors — governments, veto players, and EU institutions — sit on ideological scales, following the Agenda Setting Power Model (Jahn 2018). All data comes from the PIP – Parties, Institutions & Preferences dataset and every calculation runs in your browser.

Ideological scales

LR
Left–Right — the general economic/social left–right position.
GG
Green–Growth — ecology versus economic growth.
RILE
the classic Manifesto-Project right–left index.

The three tabs

Country models
Per country, the government position and the veto-player range on the chosen scale, quarter by quarter. Standard results appear instantly; changing a country's model (how the government is aggregated, minority rule, which veto players count) recomputes just that country live.
Party positions
Where each individual party sits over time. Pick LR or GG to get that scale's full set of Jahn indices — position, importance (how much weight the party gives the dimension), the narrower Core and wider Plus variants with their importances, and cohesion. RILE is offered as a single extra column.
EU institutions
Positions of the European Council, Commission, Council of Ministers, Parliament, and the overall EU, modelled per treaty period. You may swap the institutional models.

Choosing your own model — what this adds over a plain download

Every result starts from a standard model: the operationalization used in the PIP publications, chosen on the basis of the relevant theories and, for the government models, on an empirical analysis (Jahn 2018). These defaults are precomputed, so the published measures appear instantly without your having to touch a single setting.

But an operationalization is a theoretical decision, not a fact. Others may prefer a different one, and a specific research question may call for a different emphasis. Every such decision is therefore exposed as a control you can change — the result is then recomputed live, for that country alone:

Government
How the government's position is aggregated: weighted by seats, by cabinet members or by portfolios, as an unweighted mean, or as the position of the Prime Minister, of a specific minister (any of 18 portfolios), of the PM negotiating with that minister, or under unanimity.
Minority cabinets
Whether a minority government is treated like any other cabinet, or as a minimal connected winning coalition together with its support parties.
Veto players
Which actors count as a constraint — government, first chamber, second chamber, president — each switched on or off to match your own definition of a veto player. The EU can be added on top as a further veto player (VP_RANGE_EU).
EU institutions
The decision rule applied to each body per treaty period: qualified majority (fixed or rotating presidency), mean, median, unanimity, bargaining, or power weights.
The scale itself
LR, GG, RILE — or one you define yourself under Custom (see below).

This is what separates the tool from a static data download: you are not tied to one author's operationalization, and you can see straight away how much a finding depends on it.

Custom scale

Every tab has a fourth scale option, Custom, alongside LR/GG/RILE: build your own from manifesto categories, e.g. per501 - per110 (click “show available codes” for the full labelled list). It shows party positions in the Party tab, the government position in Country models, and the EU institutions' positions in EU institutions — always computed live, since there is no precomputed standard model for a scale you just typed in.

Reading a result

A higher value sits further right / toward growth; a lower value further left / toward ecology. GOV_POS is the government's position. VP_RANGE is the ideological spread of the chosen national veto players (a wider range means change is harder to pass); VP_RANGE_EU is the same range with the EU added as an extra veto player — it can only be equal to or wider than VP_RANGE, and only differs during actual EU membership. Comparing the two shows how much the EU constrains policy change on top of national veto players. MISFIT = EU_POS − GOV_POS shows how far a member state's government sits from the EU position.

Getting the data out

Toggle Quarterly / Yearly, then download any table as CSV, Excel, Stata or R. The first “custom” calculation loads the R engine once (~30 s); after that it is quick.
